# Mentoring session brief: Ravenna Chen Yaohui (CEO)

**15 minutes.** We are four students from IT and software backgrounds, with no business or pitching
experience. We are one of roughly 100 teams answering the same Domain 1 problem statement. We
want her honest view on three things: **is the problem real, is our solution solid, and how do we
pitch it to win a place in the final.**

## 1. The story (about 45 seconds)

> **Team: replace this with a real person if you can.** Talk to two or three people this week (a
> relative in banking, a friend in shared services, an HR officer) and use their words, with their
> permission. Until then, tell it as an *example* that is built from the facts below, not as a real
> person.

*Example:* Sofia has spent four years building automations for her company: robots that fill in
forms and check invoices. This year, AI agents started doing the same work in minutes. Her manager
says "upskill", and the training portal lists hundreds of courses. Which ones matter? Will they still matter next
year? Who pays? She has six hours a week, and no one can tell her where to start.

Sofia is not alone:

- About **1 in 4 financial-sector workers** in Malaysia are expected to be affected by automation
  within three years (AICB MyMahir workforce survey, July 2025, cited by Bank Negara Malaysia).
- **59% of Malaysia's Global Business Services workforce** (about 163,000 people) need upskilling,
  but only **37% of organisations** run active internal AI training (government-commissioned GBS
  study and AGOS Asia / Roland Berger, 2025).
- **56%** of Malaysian professionals use AI at work; only **26%** have been trained for it
  (Hays, 2025).

The problem is not a lack of courses. It is knowing **what to learn next, why, and whether it fits
your time and budget**, and having an answer that stays current as AI keeps changing.

## 2. What Adept does

Upload a CV, and Adept shows, in minutes:

1. **Which of your skills are rising or falling**, from weekly AI research and job-market data, with
   the source and date shown.
2. **Which future roles fit you**, with a score you can see inside (skills you have, demand,
   interests) and the exact skills you are missing.
3. **A step-by-step learning plan** from real courses that fits your hours and budget, including
   free and HRD Corp-claimable ones.
4. **How it connects to the UN Sustainable Development Goals**, which can even change the ranking.

For employers, the same engine shows **where the team is short**, lets HR send a **funded learning
programme**, and lets each employee **accept or ask for changes**. Employees choose what HR can see.

**The loop we think is new:** new data arrives each week → skills forecasts update → role matches
update → plans update → progress and evidence flow back to the employer. Existing tools cover pieces
of this. MyMahir (TalentCorp) shows emerging roles and training; LinkedIn Learning and Coursera sell
courses; chatbots give advice without sources. **We have not found one that closes the whole loop and
shows its evidence at every step.** We want her to test that claim.

**Design thinking in one line:** we started from the decision people struggle with ("what should I
learn next, and can I afford it?"), rejected the "ask a chatbot" idea because it invents jobs and
courses, and built a tool that shows its sources so a person can check it and decide.

*Built so far:* working web app, individual and HR modes, English / Bahasa Melayu / Chinese,
110 skills, 31 future roles, 98 verified courses, 487 automated tests.

*Data:* everything Adept reasons with is **public and real**: AI research papers (arXiv), labour-market
projections (US Bureau of Labor Statistics), occupations (O*NET), the official UN SDG list, and 98 real
courses checked against the providers' own pages. **Only the people are made up**: the CVs and personas
we demo with and the sample HR team, because the competition rules forbid real personal data.

## 3. What we don't know yet (say this openly)

- Who would pay for it: workers, employers or universities.
- Whether HR managers would trust the recommendations enough to spend budget on them.
- We have not tested it with real users.

## 4. Questions to ask her (most important first)

**Is the problem real, and who feels it most?**

1. From your experience running a company, is "what should my people learn next" a **real, painful
   problem**, or a nice-to-have? Who in a company feels it most: the employee, HR or the CEO?
2. How would you **check the problem is real** in two weeks with no budget? Who should we talk to?

**Is the solution solid as a business?**

3. When you look at a new app or idea, **what do you look at first**? What makes you take it
   seriously, and what makes you stop listening?
4. **Who pays first**: employers through the HRD Corp levy, universities, or individuals? What
   would make a company say yes to a three-month pilot?
5. TalentCorp's **MyMahir** and course platforms already exist. Is our "closed loop" a strong enough
   difference, or how would you sharpen it?
6. The hackathon sponsors are businesses (Aiversecity, Replit, Runpod). How do we frame Adept so it
   reads as an **idea a sponsor could build or invest in**, not only a student project?

**How do we win against about 100 teams with the same problem?**

7. Most teams will build "upload a CV, get course recommendations". How do we make sure the judges
   **remember us** after watching many similar videos?
8. Which part should we **lead with**: Sofia's story, the live demo, or the employer angle?

**How do you pitch?**

9. How do you usually **structure a pitch** to investors or clients? What do you say in the first
   30 seconds?
10. We are engineers and tend to explain *how* it works. How much **technical detail** belongs in a
    business pitch, and where?
11. For our **5–7 minute pitch video and slide deck**, what mistakes do technical teams make? How
    much text per slide is too much?
12. How do we talk about **impact and numbers honestly** when we have no real users yet?

**Her experience**

13. What do you wish you had known about pitching when you started? Is there anyone we should
    talk to next?
